Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

Design is primarily the art of leveraging the consumer’s psychology to encourage consumption, action, or thought. Therefore the importance of utilizing techniques in the interest of pro-social behavior is paramount as design operate on a subconscious, and visceral level first and foremost. This translates across mediums like the internet, more specifically social media, flyers, television adverts, signs, etc. Notably consideration of ethics must go into the medium just as much as the design itself as techniques like AI content generation, and predatory data-driven advertisement utilize mediums that operate at a high cost to the environment, and human security. The actual language contained therein is what transforms art into a message, and effective rhetoric has the power for spurring behavior shifts. For example fascist rhetoric, using motifs of futurism and etherization play on fear and isolation to create extremely unethical outcomes. At the same time environmentalist movements can leverage fear, relatability, and moral urgency to build progressive coalitions for change and inclusivity.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

Design principles like contrast alignment, repetition, and proximity, and the various elements of color, font, shape, etc. that are contained within shape the reception of a given work. Projects like the UX Design field research portion, and “good design” flier showed me how thoughtful design is both received subjectively, but also draws on predictable preferences especially in familiar demographics. The opportunity to present information both to survey participants and classmates alike gave me insight into managing reactions through design choices, for example ordering the slides so that designs correlating to specific emphasis or lack thereof on principles like repetition and proximity produce an overwhelmingly positive reaction in contrast to a prior lackluster reaction. I also learned that designs need consider principles like contrast not only within themselves, but within their respective fields as you can utilize contrast to stand-out among competitors and peers even if it would not be standardly seen as part of a given design.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

The course has been designed to stimulate conversations over trouble-shooting, efficiency, effectiveness, and overall experience of design across multiple mediums. The small-group work has taught me how to take technical language that goes without saying, like using a two-finger click, or cmd on a Mac as opposed to ctrl on windows, and translate that into useful , and sometimes useless directives. Getting access to immediate feedback as to how effective those directions are helped me tailor some of the language I use, and allowed me to adopt technical language and understanding from my peers who knew better than me across many of the projects. The collective revision process made the burden of learning new technologies feel much lighter, and taught me how to navigate sharing that burden with others. In some instances we were able to leverage knowledge of Adobe, and then someone who knew enough to understand it at first glance would then utilize their knowledge of cross-platform application, and then someone who was already done would work ahead to communicate troubleshooting opportunities.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Especially without prior knowledge of design principles and software revision has been an interactive process from day one of this course. Initially I understood revision as working towards personal satisfaction with a piece, I come from literature so this meant revising to better communicate my ideas to a given audience. What wasn’t considered was either the medium, or the diversity of potential audiences. The UX design project was probably the single biggest point of mindset shift as the logo I thought was personally the cleanest was ranked cleanly in last by the three interviewees. I then had to broaden my technical knowledge of adobe to implement the recommended changes that were made to me. Similarly the revision process on the good design project was interesting because in addition to being about personal taste it also begged the question of designing for a septic form, and forcing myself to re-articulate things in different, more concise, or even completely different way to meet the constraints of my design, and then to meet the constraints of its reception to others. The anonymous posting board was instrumental because although I was personally content with a sentence in my design I realized the ideas communicated were nonsense absent context only I had, and it needed to be shifted both grammatically, and on a more general level. Overall the course has expanded my conception of revision on two levels, technical dimensions required to thoroughly implement ideas, and external constraints of reception placed upon a given project.

5. Learn to present your work.

This class focused on two aspects of presentation to me. There was firstly the use of space and the physical setting to better engage the crowd. This struck me as new and effective as in debate and high school I had only ever presented at a podium, and many mannerisms like projecting my voice, hand movements, and cadence only work in a stationery setting, but instead I could play on different ways of engaging with people. One example that stands out to me the was presentation on the UX design project, it was set in a small room and I was able to lean away from the board, and move towards the small number of students in the room almost as if to experience the design with them. That fundamentally changed the reception of the logos because instead of being told how or what to feel there was a moment of asking them to look with me that made it that much more engaging and organic. Similarly the initial presentation on design Fuego forced me to break some of my habits from debate of over explaining, and quickening my speaking pace to get more information across, because in a setting with more freedom the form of information matters much much more as no one is expected to take notes. So secondarily I learned better ways to mechanistically engage an audience, as standing still and talking fast was visibly poorly received. I was able to take some of those ideas and sow it down, at one point in the UX design presentation I was able to use a moment of collective annoyance at technical jargon to build sympathy with the audience, and speak some of their thoughts into existence about overcomplicating, and redundancy. The course has forced me to adapt to real audiences as opposed to captive and already invested audiences I was used to.
